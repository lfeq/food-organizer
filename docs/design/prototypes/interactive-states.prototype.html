Force state real :hover / :active work too — and Tab moves focus

PROTOTYPE — What do the interactive states look like?

Issue #65 on map #31. The mockup is eleven static artboards and shows no hover, pressed, disabled or focus-visible state anywhere. Three state languages below — three answers to "what changes when you touch a control" — each applied to the same component set, so the decision is made once for the system rather than seven times for seven buttons.

Two constraints shape all three. The design has no shadows, so the usual lift-on-hover is unavailable: state must live in ground, border, or ink. And phone and desktop are both primary, so hover can never be the only signal that something is a control — every language below must also answer with a pressed state that a thumb can feel.

RESOLVED. B — Border and ink, focus ring F1, disabled D3, and the day card is not a control. Losing boards are kept below on purpose, so the reasoning stays legible. The decision lives in docs/design/visual-system.md under Interactive states, not here.

Focus-visible and disabled are decided separately, in their own rows at the bottom: they are orthogonal to the hover language, and each has an accessibility floor the hover choice does not.

The three state languages

Same components, same tokens, same markup in every column. Anything you see differ is the language itself. Use the Force state bar to pin all three columns in the same state at once.

A — Ground steps
One rule for everything: touching a control steps its ground one notch, pressing steps it two. Borders and ink never move. Transparent controls acquire a ground they didn't have.
Cost: on warm paper with no shadows, a 4% wash is nearly invisible — and on the green and dark fills the step is close to unreadable.

B — Border and ink CHOSEN
The ground never moves; the border does the work. --rule-control is promoted to --rule-strong, faint ink darkens to --ink, and pressing sinks the ground to --ground-sunken. Filled buttons have no border to promote, so they use the spec's own --accent-hover and a lightened inverse.
This is the spec's own language already: --rule-strong means "emphasis, not decoration" on the today card and the focused field. It generalises that one rule.

C — Accent tint
Green is the "live" colour: touching anything tints it, borrowing the segmented control's selected treatment (accent border over --ground-accent-soft).
Cost, and the reason it is here: it breaks the spec's biggest decision — green acts on the weekly plan, dark acts on the catalogue — by turning Add dish and every catalogue row green on touch. It also sprays a colour the spec calls "a 2–5% colour" across the whole screen.

Focus ring — its own decision

Orthogonal to the hover language, and the one state with a hard accessibility floor: WCAG 2.2 asks for a focus indicator at least 2px thick, contrasting 3:1 against what it replaces. Shown here permanently, not on focus, so all three are comparable at a glance. The design has no shadows, so F3's ring is a double outline, not a glow.

F1 — Accent ring, 2px, offset 2 CHOSEN
--accent on --ground-page is 5.8:1, well clear of the 3:1 floor. Reads as "the app is talking to you", and ties focus to the same green as the active tab indicator.
F2 — Ink ring, 2px, offset 2
Full ink, the strongest mark in the system, and consistent with the focused field going to full ink — but against a dark button it is nearly invisible at the boundary.
F3 — Ink ring with a paper gap
2px of page ground, then 2px of ink. Survives on any ground, dark fills included, at the cost of the heaviest mark of the three.

Disabled — its own decision

Where this actually appears: Generate week with an empty catalogue, and Save dish with an empty name. Disabled controls are exempt from the WCAG contrast minimum, so this is a legibility and honesty judgement rather than a compliance one.

D1 — Uniform opacity .4
One rule, every control, no new tokens. Drops the green button to 1.8:1 against the page and the dark one to 2.5:1 — both faint enough that the two stop being distinguishable as actions.
D2 — Flatten to the chip greys
A disabled control loses its identity: --ground-chip under --ink-muted. No new tokens either — it reuses the idle chip. Green and dark become indistinguishable when off.
D3 — Fade fills, hollow the outlines CHOSEN
Filled buttons fade to .55 (green lands at 2.4:1, still legible as a shape); outlined ones keep full opacity and drop to muted ink over a lighter rule, so the border does not go mushy.

The question none of the columns answers on its own

1. The phone has no hover at all. Whatever wins, every control also needs a pressed state that survives without one — carried into the spec as a rule: a control with a hover and no pressed state is a bug. The ↻/→ half of this was already answered by Decide the palette's accessibility floor, which ruled that a glyph forming the whole control takes real ink and never --ink-faint; this prototype still draws the pre-#63 --ink-faint reroll glyph.

2. Should a whole day card be tappable? Decided: no. All three columns above still assume yes and give the card a hover — that is the losing board, kept. Only the ↻ is interactive; the day card takes no state at all. Generalised in the spec as: a surface that merely contains a control is not itself a control.
